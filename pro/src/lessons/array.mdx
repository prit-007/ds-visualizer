# Arrays: Contiguous Memory

## Why it matters

An array packs values into consecutive addresses, so the machine can jump straight to slot *i*
with a little arithmetic — no walking, no following links. That cheap jump is why reading index
`i` is O(1), and it is also why inserting in the middle is expensive: everything after the hole
must slide one address to the right.

## How it works

1. **Index becomes an address:** `address = base + i × elementSize`.
2. **Push** writes at the end — amortized O(1) until the block is full and must be reallocated.
3. **Insert at position** shifts the tail right, then writes — O(n) moves, narrated step by step.
4. **Remove at position** shifts the tail left to close the gap — also O(n).
